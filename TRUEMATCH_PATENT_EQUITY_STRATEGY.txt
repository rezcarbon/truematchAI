### OPTION B: WARRANT/OPTION STRUCTURE (MIDDLE GROUND)

Structure:
  - Investor gets standard seed equity (SAFE/convertible note)
  - PLUS warrant to acquire X% of patents at specified valuation
  - Warrant exercisable if company reaches milestones (revenue, Series A)

Example:
  - Peak XV invests $1M for 19% of company at $5M post
  - PLUS warrant to buy 5% of patents at $2M patent valuation
  - Warrant vests/exercisable at Series A or $100K ARR

Benefits:
  ✅ Simpler structure (one cap table, one entity)
  ✅ Motivates investor involvement (want to hit milestones to exercise)
  ✅ De-risks company (investor has upside even if patents never licensed)
  ✅ Aligns incentives (investor wants patents to be valuable)

Risks:
  ❌ Less immediate patent upside (warrant not exercised until later)
  ❌ Accounting complexity (warrant valuation for financial statements)
  ❌ Timing uncertainty (when can warrants be exercised?)

---

### OPTION C: PURE EQUITY SHARE OF PATENTS (SIMPLEST)

Structure:
  - Patents remain in main company (TrueMatch Inc)
  - Investor equity includes proportional patent ownership
  - No separate entity; all one cap table

Example:
  - Peak XV invests $1M for 19% of TrueMatch (includes patents)
  - 19% ownership = 19% of company revenue + 19% of patent licensing

Benefits:
  ✅ Simple: Single cap table, straightforward
  ✅ Clear: Patent ownership proportional to company ownership
  ✅ Tax efficient: Single entity structure

Risks:
  ❌ Limited upside: Patent value dilutes with company equity dilution
  ❌ Licensing complications: Need investor approval for patent licensing deals
  ❌ Risk concentration: Patents tied to company fate

PART 7: PATENT EQUITY RISKS & MITIGATIONS
================================================================================

### RISK #1: INVESTOR CONFLICTS OVER PATENT STRATEGY
**Risk:** Multiple investors with patent equity disagree on licensing decisions

Mitigation:
  - Establish clear governance: Licensing deals <$100K = CEO decision
  - >$100K licensing requires investor approval (majority of patent stakeholders)
  - Quarterly sync calls with patent shareholders (alignment)
  - Pro-rata participation rights (investor can co-invest in patent licensing)

---

### RISK #2: PATENT VALUATION DISPUTES
**Risk:** Investors argue patents worth more/less than stated $1.5M-$2.5M

Mitigation:
  - Get independent valuation (third-party IP firm)
  - Document assumptions (cost to develop, cost to design-around, licensing comps)
  - Make valuation conservative (understate to avoid disputes)
  - Allow for valuation adjustments at Series A (true-up if company succeeds)

---

### RISK #3: PATENT LICENSING UNLIKELY
**Risk:** LinkedIn, Workday, competitors don't actually license patents

Mitigation:
  - Don't oversell patent licensing upside (position as "potential upside, not guaranteed")
  - Focus on patent defensive value (prevents competitor entry)
  - Show patent cost-to-design-around ($2M-$5M)
  - Explain regulatory moat (EU AI Act makes competitors NEED compliance)
  - Patent value independent of licensing (can be acquired, sold, licensed)

---

### RISK #4: COMPLEXITY DETERS INVESTORS
**Risk:** Patent structure too complex; investors prefer simple equity

Mitigation:
  - Keep structure simple: 5% of patents, straightforward calculation
  - One-page explainer: "Here's how patent equity works"
  - Compare to existing models: "Similar to warrant structures VCs understand"
  - Emphasize simplicity: "You own 5% of patents, same cap table as company"

---

### RISK #5: TAX IMPLICATIONS UNCLEAR
**Risk:** Patent equity structure has unfavorable tax consequences

Mitigation:
  - Consult tax attorney (confirm structure is favorable)
  - Document IP transfer (patents assigned to PatentCo at cost basis)
  - Ensure investor understands tax treatment before signing
  - Patent equity valuation for 409A (for common stock valuation)
