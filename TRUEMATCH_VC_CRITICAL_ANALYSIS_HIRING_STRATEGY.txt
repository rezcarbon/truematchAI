### CONCERN #2: PATENT EQUITY ADDS COMPLEXITY (SEVERITY: 7/10)

**What you're proposing:**
- Separate PatentCo holding company
- Investors get 5% of patents
- New hires get 1% of patents each
- Multiple cap tables to manage

**My concern as VC:**

"Now I have to track TWO cap tables. Company cap table AND patent cap table.
Every financing round, I need to update both. Every employee option grant, I need
to decide: do they get company equity, patent equity, or both?

More importantly: **What happens if the patents are never licensed?**

You're telling your investors 'patents are worth $1.5M-$2.5M.' But what if:
- LinkedIn designs around the gates (they have 10,000 engineers)
- EU AI Act rules change (patents become table-stakes feature)
- Patent office rejects key claims (prosecution fails)
- Competitors license patents cheap (licensing revenue = $0)

Then your $1.5M-$2.5M patent valuation is ZERO. And I own 5% of zero.

**Here's what I prefer:**

Keep it simple: One cap table. Company owns everything (company + patents).
Investors get one class of equity. When patents license, that's company revenue
(benefit all shareholders equally). No separate patent equity complexity.

You can mention patent upside in pitch ('our patents have licensing potential'),
but don't make it a separate asset class. **Separation = governance nightmare.**"

**VC Recommendation: Simplify to single cap table; mention patents as upside, not separate equity**

### CONCERN #3: FOUNDER-LED SALES IS UNDERESTIMATED (SEVERITY: 7/10)

**What you're proposing:**
- Hire VP Sales immediately (first hire post-seed)
- Let them build playbook
- Assume sales happens faster with experienced VP

**My concern as VC:**

"I've backed 50 seed-stage companies. Here's what I learned:

**Founder-led sales beats hired VP Sales 70% of the time in early stage.**

Why? Because:
1. Founders know the product better than anyone
2. Founders have conviction that VP Sales doesn't have
3. VP Sales expects mature sales infrastructure (CRM, marketing, demos)
4. VP Sales want to build teams (expensive before PMF)
5. VP Sales often SILO the sales process (cuts out founder learning)

**Better path:**

1. Keep Rez leading sales for first 6-12 months
2. Get to $100K MRR (or 3 pilot LOIs) with founder-led sales
3. THEN hire VP Sales (now sales is proven; VP comes in to scale)
4. VP Sales role: Build team + scale repeatable playbook (not prove sales works)

By doing this, you:
- Save money (Rez doesn't need extra salary; he's already working)
- Retain control (founder runs sales, learns the customer)
- Reduce risk (prove sales before paying someone $150K/year + 2.5% equity)
- Negotiate better (when you hire VP Sales, you have proof it works)

**Your current plan: Hire VP Sales to PROVE sales works**
**Better plan: Founder proves sales works, THEN hire VP Sales to scale**

This is how Airbnb, DoorDash, Stripe did it (founders sold first, hired specialists
second)."

**VC Recommendation: Have Rez lead sales for 6-12 months; hire VP Sales AFTER proving it works**

### CONCERN #5: FOUR FOUNDERS = GOVERNANCE NIGHTMARE (SEVERITY: 6/10)

**What you're proposing:**
- Rez + Carmen (co-founders)
- VP Sales (new co-founder)
- VP Product (new co-founder)
- Total: 4 co-founders

**My concern as VC:**

"Four co-founders is a disaster waiting to happen. Here's why:

**Decision-making becomes slow:**
- 2 founders: Can make decisions quickly (tie-break method is simple)
- 4 founders: Need consensus; if 2-2 split, who decides?
- Example: VP Sales wants to focus on enterprise; VP Product wants mid-market
  - Both make good arguments
  - Rez breaks tie by voting with Carmen
  - VP Sales feels overruled (leaves)

**Equity conflicts:**
- VP Sales gets 2.5%; think they deserve 4%
- VP Product gets 2.5%; think they deserve 3.5%
- Rez + Carmen think they deserve more (they built MVP)
- Everyone upset = dysfunction

**Board dynamics:**
- With seed investor, you might have 4-5 board seats
- All 4 co-founders want board seat
- Or investor wants only 1-2 founders on board
- Who gets left off? Resentment

**Historical precedent:**
- Successful 4-founder companies: RARE
- Successful 2-founder companies: COMMON (Facebook, Google, Airbnb)
- By Series A: Most companies have removed 1-2 co-founders

**I've seen this movie:**

Company X hired 2 co-founders pre-seed. All 4 got along during MVP phase.
At Series A, CEO seat was contested (all 4 wanted CEO role).
Ended up removing 1 founder entirely (and their 2.5% equity went to litigation).
Spent $500K on lawyers instead of sales.

**Better structure:**
Keep Rez as sole CEO. Hire VP Sales + VP Product as senior executives (not co-founders).
- VP Sales: 1-1.5% equity + VP title (not co-founder)
- VP Product: 1-1.5% equity + VP title (not co-founder)
- Rez/Carmen make final decisions (no tie-break conflicts)
- Clear reporting structure (VPs report to Rez)

This is what Sequoia recommends. This is what works."

**VC Recommendation: Hire VPs as executives, not co-founders; keep founder count at 2**
